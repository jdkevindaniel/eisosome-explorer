🎯 Teacher challenge Ask: “What do you predict will happen if we remove all the Sur7-family proteins but keep Pil1?”
The furrow becomes much more strongly invaginated and can curl into tube-like structures. That is what the 5× Sur7-family deletion produced in the paper.
Science underneath the cartoon. In budding yeast, MCC/eisosomes are stable plasma-membrane furrows. Pil1/Lsp1 BAR-domain proteins form a coat on the inner membrane surface and promote inward curvature. Haase et al. found that Sur7-family tetraspanners form strands near the furrow edges. Removing all five tested Sur7-family members produced strongly invaginated, tube-like Pil1 structures; re-anchoring Sur7 could rescue associated defects. This sandbox is a conceptual teaching model, not a quantitative physical simulation.
Based on Haase et al., EMBO Reports 24:e57232 (2023).
Typical MCC/eisosome scale reported: ~200–400 nm long, 30–50 nm wide, 50–100 nm deep.
